## Description in 50 characters or fewer

AI that fills job applications with your approval

## What are you making?

Screenmate is a Chrome extension that helps job seekers complete applications on employer websites. It fills saved details and drafts answers using the applicant's background and the role. It checks that changes actually reached the form, asks the user for missing personal facts or decisions, and leaves review and final submission to the applicant.

## Product progress

I built a working prototype with form scanning, profile-based filling, written-answer drafting, and checks that each change reached the page. The product also has a web workspace for saving a profile and reviewing CV imports. The demo shows the extension completing a controlled sample workflow. My next step is testing with job seekers on real applications, measuring time saved and corrections needed, and learning whether they return.

## Recognition

Screenmate placed third globally out of 687 submitted projects at AI Tinkerers' Agents Everywhere Hackathon with OpenAI. I represented Atlanta, the only U.S. chapter among the top three.

## Problem and product insight

Job applications repeatedly ask for information a candidate already has, then add questions specific to the role. Copying context and answers between a form and an AI chat creates more work. Screenmate brings the applicant's background into the application page and verifies its changes so the user can review the result in place.

## Competitors and approach

Alternatives include Simplify, Tsenta, manual entry, and a general AI assistant. Simplify already offers autofill and tailored written answers, and Tsenta offers broader job-search automation. Screenmate focuses on the application the person is currently reviewing: visible changes, checks that values reached the form, and user control over personal decisions and submission. I still need to establish with users whether this workflow gives them a reason to choose Screenmate.

## Technical description, if requested

The workspace and backend use Next.js, React, and TypeScript. A Chrome extension reads form controls, writes values through browser events, and reads them back to check the result. The backend handles model requests through OpenRouter and optional company research through Exa. Generated answers receive a separate review against the saved applicant background before automatic filling.
